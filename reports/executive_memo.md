# Executive memo

## U.S. airline financial and operating performance, 2025

The four largest U.S. passenger carriers generated more revenue than in 2019, but none recovered its pre-pandemic operating margin. The gap is most pronounced at Southwest, where 2025 operating margin remained 11.7 percentage points below 2019.

### Findings

1. **Delta led the peer group on operating profitability.** Revenue reached $63.4 billion and operating margin was 9.2%. Delta also reported the lowest fuel-cost share of revenue at 15.5% and reduced net debt to $8.8 billion.

2. **United produced the strongest operating cash flow margin.** Its 14.3% operating cash flow margin exceeded Delta's 13.2%, while its 8.0% operating margin ranked second. International passengers represented 24.8% of reported traffic, the highest share in the peer group.

3. **American's capital structure limits room for error.** Revenue was 19.4% above 2019, but operating margin was 2.7% and net debt remained $24.2 billion. A simple 15% fuel-cost sensitivity would reduce operating income from $1.47 billion to approximately negative $0.14 billion before hedging or management actions.

4. **Southwest's main issue is the combination of utilization and margin.** Its 2025 load factor was 74.4%, compared with 83.0% at Delta. Operating margin was 1.5%, down 11.7 percentage points from 2019. The same fuel-cost sensitivity would reduce operating margin below zero.

### Management implications

- Delta and United have the greatest capacity to absorb a fuel-cost increase without moving into an operating loss.
- American should prioritize debt reduction and margin improvement over capacity growth that does not improve unit economics.
- Southwest needs a measurable utilization and revenue-quality plan. Capacity decisions should be evaluated against load factor, fare realization and operating margin together.
- Monthly monitoring should combine passengers, seats, fuel cost and operating margin. A single traffic measure does not explain financial performance.

### Sensitivity note

The fuel case increases reported 2025 fuel cost by 15% while holding revenue, traffic, non-fuel operating costs and hedging effects constant. It is a sensitivity analysis, not company guidance.
